I Never Thought About My Septic Tank Until an Excavator Showed Up Next Door

Watching my neighbor’s yard get dug up was the wake-up call I needed.

Updated August 2026

The beeping started early one morning.

I looked outside and saw an excavator in my neighbor’s yard. Workers were standing around a deep hole near the house.

At first, I thought they were putting in a pool.

Then I saw the septic truck.

My neighbor later told me his septic system had a serious problem. By the time he knew something was wrong, digging up the yard was his only choice. The work was going to cost thousands of dollars.

Our homes were built around the same time. That made me wonder: What did I know about my own system?

The honest answer was almost nothing.

I Thought “No Problem” Meant “Good Condition”

Our toilets flushed. The drains worked. The yard did not smell.

So I thought everything was fine.

But most of a septic system is buried. You cannot see the tank fill up. You cannot see what is happening in the drainfield. It is easy to forget about it until something goes wrong.

I started looking for simple ways to care for it. I found a lot of mixed advice about pumping, bacteria, bleach, water use, and additives.

Some pages made septic care sound hard. Others said one product could fix everything.

Neither answer felt right.

The Boring Advice Was the Most Helpful

The EPAsays most systems should be checked every one to three years and pumped about every three to five years. The right timing depends on your home, tank, water use, and amount of waste.

I also learned that what goes down the drain matters.

Wipes, grease, paper towels, trash, and harsh waste can cause trouble. Fixing leaks and spreading laundry through the week can also help.

I wasn't looking for a magic fix. I wanted to stop ignoring my septic system.
